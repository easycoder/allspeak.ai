## Notes on the drafting choices

- **Names in the first line.** Thanks to the Director *and* acknowledgement of the two colleagues, in that order. It confirms the handoff has been read and accepted.
- **"the call for contributions, well before the February deadline"** mirrors the recipient's own wording rather than introducing the term "questionnaire", which appears only on the linked UNESCO page and not in the email itself. Naming a deadline they did not mention shows the call has actually been read — but note UNESCO's own pages disagree on the exact day (27th on the monitoring page, 28th on the news article), so the email says "February" rather than a date.
- **"in a form that can be cited"** signals that a published contribution is wanted, without appearing to angle for publicity.
- **The individual-vs-organisation question is asked, not guessed.** The questionnaire is written for organisations; the call does list the "technical community" and "other stakeholders", so an individual submission is legitimate — but asking the Secretariat costs one sentence and avoids getting it wrong.
- **The IDIL paragraph deliberately offers restraint.** Declining to submit a thin entry reads as integrity to a UN audience, and it is the truthful position. It also invites guidance rather than requiring a decision.
- **"without overclaiming what it is"** is the sentence that makes the rest credible. Officials are practised at spotting a solo developer presenting as an organisation; naming the limitation first removes their reason to discount the rest.
- **No funding request anywhere.** It was excluded from the original approach for the same reason and must stay excluded. The subject can be raised later, by them, if a programme exists.
- **Two concrete slots, plus flexibility.** Offer two specific times rather than "let me know when suits" — the former gets a calendar entry, the latter gets lost.
