## 2. The strategic opening to lead with

UNESCO's multilingualism agenda currently covers: content, interfaces and documentation, machine translation, domain names and email addresses (Universal Acceptance), and Indigenous language revitalisation. **None of it covers the programming language itself.**

That is the gap AllSpeak fills, and it is a gap these three people are unusually well placed to recognise. Frame it as: *language technologies have addressed how people read and write prose; nobody has addressed the language in which people read and write logic — the code that increasingly governs their access to services.* The AI point strengthens this, and lands better with a policy audience than with programmers: as AI writes more code, the population that must *read and verify* it is no longer limited to English-literate professionals.

Secondary framings worth having ready:

- **The 2003 Recommendation directly.** It calls for "language technologies" and "multilingual digital content" and equitable access. AllSpeak is an implementation of a specific clause — cite the clause.
- **ROAM.** "Accessibility" and "Multi-stakeholder participation" both map onto AllSpeak; Hu will hear it in those terms whether or not you use them.
- **Universal Acceptance.** The UNESCO–ICANN work is about accepting non-ASCII in domain names and addresses. The same argument applies one level deeper to source code.

## 4. The IDIL question — handle with care

The IDIL call is a **mapping exercise for planned activities**, not a funding or endorsement mechanism. AllSpeak currently has no Indigenous-language activity, and the definition of an Indigenous language is a matter for the communities concerned, not for a project to assert on their behalf.

Two honest routes:

1. **Submit a modest, accurate entry** describing AllSpeak as a capability offered to Indigenous language communities — being explicit that no such work is yet underway, and that the project would extend to a community-selected language at a community's initiative. Worth doing *only* if the form allows that framing.
2. **Ask Hu first.** "Is there a way to be listed that is accurate about this, or is the mapping only for activities already planned?" This is a reasonable question to a colleague, costs nothing, and cannot misfire.

Route 2 is the better default. A weak or overreaching entry into a UN mapping exercise buys visibility you don't need and costs credibility you do.

**Do not reframe AllSpeak as an Indigenous-language project to chase IDIL.** The honest and stronger case is broader — all speakers of non-English languages, including but not limited to Indigenous communities. Retrofitting the pitch to a funding window is exactly the kind of thing that reads as opportunism to the people you are trying to impress.

## 5. Meeting preparation

**Format:** 30 minutes, online. Offer a 5-minute live demo, and have it pre-run and offline-safe — do not debug in the meeting.

**Agenda to send in advance (three items, no more):**

1. Where AllSpeak sits relative to UNESCO's existing language-technology work, and whether there is room for it in the policy-brief pipeline.
2. How a solo, unfunded, open-source project can contribute usefully to UNESCO's processes without overclaiming.
3. What would be genuinely useful to build in the next 12 months, and whether any of it suits a pilot with a partner.

**Pre-read:** the one-page note at `documents/unesco-one-pager.md` — the gap, the working implementation, the evidence (four languages, DOI, tutorial, a deployed business system), and one explicit question, in UNESCO's vocabulary. Not the 6,200-word whitepaper.

Rebuild the printable version after any edit:

```
cd documents
python3 md-to-html.py --compact unesco-one-pager.md
wkhtmltopdf --enable-local-file-access --print-media-type unesco-one-pager.html unesco-one-pager.pdf
```

`--compact` is required — the default layout in `md-to-html.py` is the title-page style used for the whitepaper, and a brief of this length runs to two pages under it. `unesco-one-pager.pdf` is A4, one page, verified.

**Have ready, in one line each:** current status (four language packs, three awaiting native-speaker validation); the DOI; the video; the Account application as deployed proof.

**The one small ask to make:** whether UNESCO would consider including AllSpeak as a case study, or referring to it in the consultation synthesis. Do **not** ask for money in a first meeting. If an institutional need emerges later, the lever is an IFAP National Committee or a university acting as fiscal host. Godoi is IFAP Secretary, and UNESCO's IFAP National Committees page (February 2026) counts **23 active committees**, not the fifty-odd that older sources cite — so the network is real but smaller than it looks, and a university is probably the more available fiscal host.

## 6. What not to do

- **Never describe this exchange as UNESCO endorsement, support, or partnership** — in the whitepaper, on the website, or in approaches to ITU/UNDP. Ask permission before quoting anything, including internally. This is the one mistake that would end the relationship.
- **Don't treat a positive reply as validation of the project's premise.** It validates that one Director found it interesting.
- **Don't overclaim in the questionnaire.** It is read by Secretariat staff compiling a synthesis. Answer in their vocabulary, describe activities and outcomes in the 2023–2027 window, cite the DOI, and stay understated. A contribution that reads as marketing is worse than no contribution.
- **Be accurate about the 2023–2027 window.** AllSpeak's lineage predates the 2026 fork; the longevity is an asset, but present it accurately.
- **Don't let the end of February 2027 become a February rush.** UNESCO's own pages give 27 and 28 February; December–January is the Secretariat's slow period; submit by early January so there is time to answer any request for clarification.
- **Don't change the product to please the audience.** The UN-facing track (consultations, briefs) and the build track stay separate.
